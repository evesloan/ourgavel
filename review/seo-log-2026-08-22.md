## What this run changed

### E9 — the page titled "what each verdict means" did not say what each verdict means

`/cases/<slug>/standard/` is titled *The law, in plain English* and its meta description has
always promised "what the jury must decide, who has to prove what, and what each verdict means."
It carried the first two. The third was on a different page, inside a collapsed `<details>` fold
on the case hub.

The data was already there and already sourced. `verdictOptions` on each case gives every outcome
the jury can return with the sentencing exposure attached, cited to the statute: three sentencing
options plus two consecutive enhancements under NRS 200.030 / 193.165 / 193.168 for Davis;
mandatory life with no parole and no judicial discretion under Fla. Stat. § 775.082(1)(a) for
Fernandez; a mandatory life term under 18 U.S.C. § 1958 with federal parole abolished in 1987 for
Durk, plus a § 924 count that must run consecutively.

That block now renders on the explainer under `<h2 id="verdicts">What each verdict means</h2>`,
with its sources, on all five cases. The hub fold is untouched — a reader scanning the case still
gets it there. Two pages sharing one sourced section is normal; degrading the hub to avoid it
would trade a reader's convenience for a crawler's tidiness, which is backwards.

*Why this and not a new URL:* a `/verdict/` page would have been the same five paragraphs at a
fresh address, which is a doorway page with better manners. Putting the answer on the page that
already claims it costs nothing and fakes nothing.

*Competitor check first.* NewsNation ranks for "Lindsay Clancy trial: what each possible verdict
would mean" and the Boston Globe has the insanity-defence mechanics. Neither builds it per case,
neither keeps it current, and neither carries the statute next to the sentence. Four of our five
cases have nobody doing it at all.

*Hypothesis:* impressions on sentencing-exposure queries — "what sentence does Duane Davis face",
"is life without parole mandatory in Florida", "what happens if the jury is hung" — where we
currently rank for nothing, because the answer was inside a `<details>` on a page about something
else.
*Measure:* Search Console → Pages, `/standard/` URLs: impressions and query set, once a baseline
exists.
*Kill criterion:* if the explainers still draw no impressions on outcome queries after four weeks
of indexed data, the content is not the problem and the page title is; that is when a title
experiment has something to test against.

### E10 — five pages per case, and two of them were linked from one sentence each

`caseNav` — the tab strip on every case page — listed Overview, The Record and The Board. The
witness index and the legal explainer were not in it. Each was reachable from exactly one link in
the body of one other page: "Looking for a specific witness?" on the record, and "The law behind
the verdict" inside a collapsed fold on the hub.

Those two pages hold the most specific answers on the site. The witness index is what answers "who
testified on day 12 of the Clancy trial" — 61 people, each with role, testimony gist and a deep
link to the day. The explainer is now what answers "what sentence does he face". Both sat one
weak link from the rest of the case.

Both are in the tab strip now, on all five pages of all five cases: Overview · The Record ·
Witnesses · The Board · The Law. The Law tab is omitted where a case has no `legalStandard`, so
the nav cannot advertise a page that does not build. `seo.test.js` now asserts every page of a
case links to every other page of that case — 100 assertions, and the check is what caught that
`witnessesPage` was still passing `caseNav(c, 'record')` and highlighting the wrong tab.

*Hypothesis:* faster recrawl and better internal PageRank flow to the two deepest page types.
*Measure:* Search Console → Crawl stats, and Pages → Discovered/Crawled for `/witnesses/` and
`/standard/` URLs.
*Kill criterion:* none worth naming. Five tabs is still a short nav, and a page that nothing links
to is a page nobody crawls twice.

### Verdict outcomes are marked up, and the markup is checked against the page

Each explainer now carries an `ItemList` of its verdict outcomes — `name` is the option,
`description` is the consequence — alongside the `FAQPage` (Clancy) or `WebPage` block it already
had. 57 JSON-LD blocks site-wide, up from 24 before this patch and 52 in the 10:07 version.

`seo.test.js` extracts every one of those strings and searches the rendered text of the same file
for it. 231 marked-up strings checked this run, up from 181. A bare `ListItem` carrying its own
name and description is a new shape, so the test learned it rather than skipping it — the earlier
version only looked at `li.item.name` and would have waved the whole verdict block through.

**A caveat worth writing down rather than quietly enjoying:** Google restricted FAQ rich results
to authoritative government and health sites in August 2023. The `FAQPage` block on the Clancy
explainer is very unlikely to draw a rich result, and E3 should not be scored as if it will. It
stays because it is true and costs nothing, and because entity markup feeds more than one
consumer. The honest expected value of the FAQ block is close to zero; the `ItemList` and
`BreadcrumbList` blocks are the ones with a live rich-result surface.
