## What this run shipped: the four non-Clancy legal explainers answer their own jurisdiction's questions

Since 2026-08-22 the four non-Clancy `legalStandard` blocks have had no `qa` array, because a
hard-coded Massachusetts Q&A was rendering under all five headings and the SEO patch strips it out.
That patch leaves those four pages with a correction note and no questions. This run wrote them,

**Every source below was fetched this session.** Nothing is cited that was not read here.

### South Carolina — Murdaugh retrial (2 questions)

1. *Why doesn't double jeopardy stop a second trial?* — because he won the reversal himself
   (*United States v. Ball*, 1896), and the one exception, reversal for insufficient evidence
   (*Burks v. United States*, 1978), never came into play: the S.C. Supreme Court granted a new
   trial on jury interference and expressly declined to reach the evidence challenges.
   New source: Constitution Annotated, *Re-Prosecution After Conviction*.
2. *Will the second trial look like the 2023 one?* — the opinion held the financial-crimes evidence
   was properly admitted to show motive, then warned the State went far past what that justified:
   roughly twelve and a half hours of testimony it described as having little to no probative value,
   at a cost in unfair prejudice, and told the trial court to restrict it next time. Verified by
   fetching the opinion (FindLaw). The official `sccourts.org` PDF is `robots.txt`-disallowed from
   this sandbox, so FindLaw's copy of the same opinion was used.

**Two questions, not three, on purpose.** The drafts for a third all restated something the `test`
block on the same page already says, and STYLE.md §9 bans a sentence that restates the sentence
above it. Two that add something beat three where one is filler.

### Nevada — Davis (3 questions)

The useful find here is a distinction our own `test` block blurs, and it is now on the page.
**Corpus delicti does not require independent evidence that Davis was there.** *Sheriff v. Dhadda*
requires independent proof of two things only: the fact of death, and that the death came about
through the criminal agency of another. Identity is the job of a different rule — NRS 175.291,
accomplice corroboration, which is the one that demands evidence tending "to connect the defendant
with the commission of the offense". A reader who conflates them will misread the whole trial.
Third question covers how much corroboration is enough, from *Gallego v. State* (1985): cumulative,
circumstantial, and it need not prove guilt by itself. New source: *Gallego*.

### Federal (C.D. Cal.) — Banks (3 questions)

Why California's AB 2799 does not reach a federal courtroom; the exact 404(b) line the judge has
been drawing song by song, quoting **404(b)(1)** and **404(b)(2)** rather than paraphrasing them;
and the point that § 1958 needs something of pecuniary value on the table, so revenge alone is not
murder-for-hire. New source: Fed. R. Evid. 404 (Cornell LII).

### Florida — Fernandez (3 questions)

How a man who was not there is guilty of the murder itself, quoting the two findings in Fla. Std.
Jury Instr. 3.5(a) and its sentence that a principal "does not have to be present when the crime is
committed"; what premeditation actually requires, quoting the instruction's "long enough to allow
reflection by the defendant"; and whether jurors must find money changed hands.

**One thing checked rather than assumed, and it changed the copy.** The draft claimed 3.5(a)
carries a "mere presence is not enough" sentence. It does not. The instruction says presence is
*unnecessary*, which is a different proposition, and the sentence would have been invented. The
answer now says only what the instruction says.

The Florida Bar's official instruction PDF returns 403 to this sandbox, so the premeditation
wording was verified against a Florida Supreme Court opinion adopting the standard instructions
(No. 82,438, 1994, via Justia), which carries the identical language. That is the source cited.
Worth re-checking against the Bar's current PDF from a machine that can reach it.

## Why a script and not a git patch

The SEO patch already edits the same four `legalStandard` blocks, adding `correction` immediately
after `sources`. Any patch of mine adding `qa` in that same place changes the context lines the SEO
patch needs, and the two cannot both apply. Four patches are already queued and two of them
collide on a `poll.js` hunk; a fifth collision was not worth creating.

`ourgavel-qa-2026-08-22.js` edits the parsed JSON instead, so it does not care which patches have
landed. It is idempotent — a second run reports `unchanged` on all four and writes nothing — and it
prints a warning if `scripts/build.js` cannot yet read `legalStandard.qa`, which is the case until
the SEO patch is in.

`JSON.stringify(doc, null, 2) + '\n'` was verified byte-identical against all five current
`case.json` files before anything was written, so the diff is the Q&A and nothing else.
